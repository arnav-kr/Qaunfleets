Allocation details

Operating assumptions Fixed rated charging power (the lower of vehicle and charger limits), with a partial final slot. Staff can relocate vehicles at slot boundaries; explicit occupied-bay events prevent charger reuse until release. Playback is simulated execution, not charger telemetry. Past slots are immutable after an event. Replanning can interrupt and resume a visit; traffic, staffing limits and relocation duration are not modelled. Confirmed dispatch times are hard constraints. An optional prediction estimates unplug behaviour, not a new dispatch commitment. The predictor was trained on workplace sessions; its use in this constructed depot is experimental.

One bit is one complete charging visit.

Start with physically valid choices. Each bit means “select this vehicle, charger and start time.” Energy and confirmed deadlines are built into every choice.

1 / Build choicesEnumerate valid visits; keep up to four per vehicle including a feasible earliest-departure visit when available, then cheap, early and late choices.
2 / Compile QUBOPrice the visits. Penalize missing/duplicate choices, charger clashes and excess shared load.
3 / Run OptSolve at each relevant peak ceiling. Keep valid returned samples and compare their actual bills.
4 / Check independentlyRecompute energy, occupancy, deadlines, site power and bill from the returned plan.

The equation we actually implement

EB(x,s) = Σ ckxk
+ A Σv(Σk∈Kᵥxk − 1)²
+ A Σ(k,l)∈Cxkxl
+ A Σt(Σ wktxk + st − Kt(B))²

For each peak ceiling B: K is the available power in exact integer units; slack s represents unused capacity. A is larger than the largest possible feasible visit cost. The outer sweep compares energy + incremental demand charges. Redundant rows are removed; simple capacity conflicts are encoded directly.

QUBO is an optimization formulation, not machine learning. This local Opt execution is classical exact search or simulated annealing. QCloud supplies the measured prediction features used by the hybrid workflow.
